Concept 1: Volleyball
Input: Player’s arm and hand movements are tracked through camera. 
What changes: Volleyball falls from top of the screen, viewer moves their arm to the ball, the ball reacts and bounces upward. Speed/force of the arm could determine speed/force or spin direction of the ball. 
How does the viewer know: The volleyball visibly reacts when it makes contact with the player's arms. A successful pass sends the ball back into the air, while missing it causes the ball to fall off the bottom of the screen.

Concept 2: Bubbles
Input: Players can use their pointer finger to pop bubbles.
What changes: bubbles appear on the screen.
How does the viewer know: If their finger makes contact with the bubble it will pop.

Concept 3: Don’t let it spill
Input: Body tilt.
What changes: A virtual glass sits in front of/on the viewer.
How does the viewer know: If they move, they can spill the glass/keep the glass from spilling. The glass may tilt on its own, causing the viewer to have to move to keep it from spilling.

Concept 4: Personal Weather
Input: Facial expressions.
What changes: Weather depending on your facial expressions. Happy=sunny. Sad=storming. etc.
How does viewer know: By changing facial expressions.

Concept 5: Fireflies
Input: opening and closing hand.
What changes: Fireflies enter the screen and viewer can catch and release them using their hands.
How does the viewer know: open hand attracts fireflies, close hand traps them, open hand releases them.

Concept 6: Makeup
Input: Touching different areas of your face
What changes: Touching your cheek, lips, eyes, etc. applies a different visual treatment to that area.
How does the viewer know: The effect appears exactly where the viewer touches their face.

Concept 7: Shadow puppets
Input: Hand shapes.
What changes: silhouette shadows form based on viewer hand shapes.
How does the viewer know: if they make a heart with their hands, a literal heart will form, etc.

Concept 8: Curtains
Input: hands moving apart and together.
What changes: scenes are revealed when curtains are moved. Different speeds could trigger different images behind the curtain. 
How does the viewer know: Pulling your hands apart. physically opens them; bringing them together closes them.

Concept 9: Spotlight
Input: Moving/dancing. 
What changes: Spotlight follows you around. There is a crowd meter that goes up and down based on viewer movement/dancing.
How does the viewer know: Crowd meter/noise.

Concept 10: Balance
Input: Arm position.
What changes: The object will fall on to the viewer's arm. The viewer has to tilt their arm up and down to keep it balanced. 
How does the viewer know: Raising one hand lowers the opposite object; leveling your hands balances both.
